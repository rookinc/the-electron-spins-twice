## Candidate geometry

Let theta be the signed twist of the receiving G60 relative to the arriving
action. The candidate register is lifted:

    -2pi <= theta <= 2pi

The full-turn states retain signed receipts even when their bare orientation
projects to the same unregistered orientation as theta equals zero.

The arriving action is separated into an exactly transverse component and a
nonorthogonal driving component:

    a = a_perpendicular + a_nonorthogonal

If the action is perfectly orthogonal, a_nonorthogonal is zero. The action may
pass through with its orientation receipt while G60 remains untwisted.

A nonzero nonorthogonal component supplies signed torsional drive.

## Candidate thresholds

At absolute(theta) equals pi, a reversal-even observable is proposed to become
available. The author identifies this candidate observable with gravity. No
physical gravity observable has yet been constructed.

At absolute(theta) equals 2pi, G60 reaches its proposed first finite twist
limit and produces its maximum permitted action. This maximum-action law is
declared by the contract and is not yet derived from a native operator.
